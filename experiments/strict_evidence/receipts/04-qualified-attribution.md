# Preserve the stroke episode and qualify the causal assessment

The R21 strict trace for assignment 817 lost two adjacent passages in the same encounter. The original narrative records a hospitalization for cryptogenic stroke on line 0. Line 1 starts “At the time” and documents CT, treatment and symptom resolution. The baseline converter produced no source-linked observation for the episode and CT. The second experiment recovered that link but inferred a final causal assessment. Independent counterexamples showed why the inference fails.

The released shared reader emits an Observation for a documented stroke episode with linked cranial imaging. Its source spans identify both passages. It emits a separate causal Observation when the note explicitly attributes the presenting symptoms after brain imaging. The shared clinical catalog binding exposes related findings even when the causal value is unknown. It also reports the remaining evidence requirement. The extractor and binding use clinical concepts, source and episode context. [Narrative reader](../../../nih_benchmark/round12/stroke_episode.py) · [FHIR binding](../../../nih_benchmark/round11/native.py)

For 817, the new resource is `Observation/x-85f4b16b0d2808b03bd690307a8135`, linked to the source DocumentReference. The exclusion for nonvascular origin after imaging executes `NOT(nonvascular_origin_after_imaging)`. The operative value is unknown and the root returns MISSING_DATA. The original note gives a hemorrhage-negative CT and a stroke diagnosis, with no documented final attribution of the presenting symptoms after that imaging. In separate fixtures an explicit final vascular assessment returns MET and an explicit nonvascular assessment returns NOT_MET. A CT plus admission diagnosis, an infarct without explicit attribution, a different patient, a withdrawn conclusion and conflicting assessments abstain.
